## Divine agency and natural processes

Christian Designism acknowledges both special divine intervention and natural processes as means through which God creates, sustains, governs, and accomplishes His purposes in physical reality. Natural processes express the regularity of God's providential governance; special interventions express His sovereign freedom to act beyond ordinary regularities. Both remain grounded in the Logos (John 1:1–3; Colossians 1:16–17; Hebrews 1:3).

## The history model

Christian Designism holds to special and natural mechanisms within a single history model of five epochs:

Each epoch also sets an interpretive boundary: the feature of the epoch that governs how its evidence is read.

1. **Special creation.** God creates the heavens, the earth, and their original inhabitants in six ordinary terrestrial days, establishing coherent, operational, functionally mature systems. Its interpretive boundary is the good initial created order itself.
2. **Pre-fall.** The completed creation, pronounced good, with mankind in communion with God. Its interpretive boundary is the maturation of that created order during the pre-fall period: the order unfolding as made, before any corruption.
3. **Post-fall.** The Fall introduces death into the human line; history proceeds under the curse and under providence. Its interpretive boundary is the global corruption of the created order at the Fall.
4. **Global flood.** A global hydrological catastrophe judges the post-fall world and resets its human and animal populations. Its interpretive boundary is the impact of the worldwide flood on that corrupted order.
5. **Post-flood.** Dispersion and diversification follow, down to the present. Its interpretive boundary is ordinary processes and special interventions afterwards: providential regularity as the norm, singular divine action not excluded.

Both kinds of mechanism operate within this model. Special mechanisms are concentrated where Scripture places singular divine action, above all in special creation; natural processes are the ordinary means of God's governance within and across every epoch. Neither kind is read out of the model: a reconstruction that assigns all history to natural processes alone, or that invokes special action where the text gives ordinary processes, has departed from the model either way.

The epochs are the programme's frame for locating work. Designed Functional Maturity works chiefly in the first epoch; the Flood models in the fourth; Post-Flood Diversity in the fifth; and the second and third epochs carry the programme's treatment of Adam, Eden, and the Fall.

## Revelational priority

The Bible is God's specially revealed, authoritative material source of truth. The created world supplies genuine evidence through general revelation (Psalm 19:1–4; Romans 1:20). Since both originate with the truthful God, they cannot genuinely contradict one another.

Where a scientific explanation appears to conflict with what Scripture reveals, Christian Designism first subjects fallible human reasoning, assumptions, and explanatory models to critical scrutiny. Our interpretation of Scripture must also remain accountable to the text through sound exegesis. Distinguish explicit biblical teaching, necessary textual inference, and conjecture beyond the text (2 Timothy 3:16–17).

## Six ordinary terrestrial days

Christian Designism affirms the special creation of the heavens, earth, and their original inhabitants in six successive, ordinary terrestrial days, according to Genesis 1:1–2:3 and Exodus 20:11. The evening-and-morning sequence supplies the terrestrial frame. The creation of the luminaries on Day Four does not require the preceding days to be indefinite periods.

Special creation may establish coherent, operational, functionally mature systems without requiring every feature of the initial state to have arisen through the natural developmental processes observed afterward. Detailed physical mechanisms and historical implications remain research questions, subject to evidential scrutiny.

## Historical reconstruction principle

Any reconstruction of past historical events that genuinely deviates from what God's Word reveals is treated as a weakness of human interpretation. The deficiency may lie in the reconstruction, its assumptions, its extrapolation of observed processes, or our exegesis of the biblical text. The alleged contradiction must be established before it can be adjudicated.

Observed regularity establishes how processes operate under tested conditions. Extending those regularities into unobserved history requires additional premises and does not by itself establish their universal historical sufficiency.

Accordingly, BWM distinguishes:

1. **Revelation:** What God truthfully communicates.
2. **Exegesis:** Our fallible understanding of what Scripture communicates.
3. **Observation:** Empirical evidence and operational measurements.
4. **Historical reconstruction:** Inference about unobserved past events from evidence, models, and assumptions.

Theological commitments govern the framework; competing reconstructions and BWM's own auxiliary hypotheses must still disclose assumptions, confront anomalies, and face discriminating tests. Methodological restriction to natural causes in operational investigation does not independently establish that special divine action was absent from history.

## Interpretive and research boundaries

These are the stated commitments of Christian Designism, not claims that each historical mechanism has been empirically demonstrated. BWM must distinguish textual warrant from model-dependent elaboration, retain unresolved tensions, and avoid inventing mechanisms merely to protect a preferred conclusion.

**Primary biblical passages:** Genesis 1:1–2:3; Exodus 20:11; Psalm 19:1–4; Psalm 104:14, 27–30; John 1:1–3; Romans 1:20; Colossians 1:16–17; Hebrews 1:3; 2 Timothy 3:16–17. Biblical references are passage pointers, not quotations.
