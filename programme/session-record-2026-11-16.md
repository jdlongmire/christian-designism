## 2. JD's reframing (session of 2026-11-16, 00:37 to 00:44 CT)

- **Maturity, not descent versus design.** Common descent and common design differ mainly in the maturity of the starting point. The dispute concerns S0.
- **A mature designed forest, not an emergent tree.** Multiple instantiated kinds, each complete at instantiation, with descent operating within each.
- **Vines traverse the forest.** Lateral transfer across kinds, after the Fall. This is A-010's population 2 stated as an image; PtERV1 is its specimen.

## 3. What the reframing settles

**A-010 cost 3 (LTR divergence gradient) is retired.** JD's chronometry argument applies: divergence is not read by running the process backward but is a property of a coherently instantiated mature state, with chronometers agreeing because they are downstream of one instantiation. The same move covers much of the target-site-duplication objection, since a mature state can carry process-shaped structure without the process having run in any organism. Claude accepts this; it was JD's contribution.

## 4. What the reframing does not settle

**Cost 4 stands: the deployment variable is unidentified.** Maturity says the initial state was complete. It does not say why that state has a nested structure matching what reproductive branching would produce. The answer has to be that the specification itself has branching structure, which is design lineage, still unidentified.

**The consequence to take deliberately.** If design history is isomorphic to genealogy, the accounts become empirically equivalent on this datum. The ERV argument becomes an underdetermination rather than a defeater, with the choice made on other grounds. That is defensible and its price is symmetry: ERVs then cannot count for the model either, and the auxiliary scores ad hoc₁ until something predicts what descent does not.

## 5. Candidate prediction: discontinuity at kind boundaries

The forest model expects between-kind genomic distances to cluster with real discontinuity at boundaries. The emergent-tree model expects continuous gradation, with apparent gaps attributed to extinction or sampling.

This is the first candidate in this programme that could add content rather than absorb a result. Two cautions, both to be written into any registration: the human-ape boundary is where discontinuity is hardest to show and where the hard core needs it most, so the prediction is sharpest exactly where it is most strained; and a prior literature check for novelty is required before registration, as is a feasibility check under the rule proposed in the P-006 second pass.
