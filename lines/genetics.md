# Line: Genetics

**Repository of record:** none. External literature line rather than an in-programme model.
**Charter section:** [§6.4](../charter/consilience-is-not-consensus.md#64-genetics)
**Predictions:** [P-004](../registry/PREDICTIONS.md#p-004--genomic-degradation-constrains-elapsed-generational-time) (`DRAFT`)

## Position

Mutational load: deleterious mutations accumulate faster than purifying selection removes them, so genomes degrade over generational time (Sanford 2005). If sustained, this constrains the duration over which extant genomes can have persisted, and the constraint runs toward shorter chronologies.

## Status against the charter

The charter's handling is correct in posture and thin in content. It records that the argument is contested within population genetics and that the contest is empirical rather than definitional, which is the right location for it and an honest thing to say about a line the programme leans on.

What the charter does not do, and what this line needs, is state the argument in a form that could fail. The load argument's force depends on selection efficiency against low-effect-size deleterious mutations, and that is a measurable quantity with an active mainstream literature arguing the opposite conclusion. A programme that cites the line without engaging that literature is not entitled to the inference.

## What registration requires

1. A quantitative statement: the selection-efficiency threshold above which the load argument fails
2. The measurement that would establish that threshold has been crossed
3. A written engagement with the principal mainstream objections, entered here, rather than a citation to Sanford alone

This is the line where the programme is most exposed to the charge of citing a favourable result without adjudicating its critics, which is the same failure the charter correctly identifies in the wider creationist literature over accelerated decay. The standard applied to A-002 applies here.
