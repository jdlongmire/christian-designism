## Position

Global Flood Hydrotectonics, currently at version 2.5 (Gap Analysis Edition). Rapid continental reorganization by hydraulic collapse: sudden failure of hydraulic seals in a fluid-rich pre-flood lithosphere, with blocks hydroplaning on thin water films along shallow detachment horizons. Dissipation is relocated from viscous shear in mantle rock into the water film, reducing heat generation by orders of magnitude while preserving rapid surface motion.

This replaces rather than refines catastrophic plate tectonics (Austin et al. 1994), whose thermal budget does not close. The two agree on outcome and disagree on mechanism.

## Interaction with the declined accelerated-decay auxiliary

Declining [A-002](../registry/AUXILIARIES.md#a-002--accelerated-nuclear-decay) does not dismiss the RATE observations, and this line has two live relationships with them. Both are recorded here because a line that only reported favourable interactions would not be worth maintaining.

**Convergence, in the programme's favour.** Polonium radiohalo formation requires rapid hydrothermal transport of extremely short-lived isotopes (Po-218 at 3 minutes, Po-214 at 164 microseconds) along fluid pathways in granite that is crystallized but still below annealing temperature. That transport network is assumed by the radiohalo argument and has not been quantified by it. The channeled-porosity architecture and Darcy-flow treatment in the hydrotectonic model is a candidate quantification. This is a contribution flowing from this programme *to* a RATE-adjacent argument, and it is the strongest such convergence identified.

**Tension, against the programme.** Helium retention in zircons is the strongest quantitative result in the RATE corpus, and it depends on the thermal and fluid history of the sampled rock. This model places a hot, fluid-saturated, hydrothermally active crust in the relevant window. Higher temperature accelerates diffusion; pervasive fluid flow supplies an advection path. Both cut against retention. A counter is available, since helium-saturated pore fluids at high pressure would change the external boundary condition, but quantifying it is this model's obligation because this model sets that boundary condition. Tracked as [I-0004](../issues/open/I-0004-helium-retention-tension.md).

A related and smaller tension: pervasive fluid circulation makes secondary carbon contamination pathways more plausible, which pushes against the in-situ reading of carbon-14 in radiocarbon-dead samples.
