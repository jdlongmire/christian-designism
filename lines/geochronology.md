## Position

Functional maturity of origins. A fiat act deploys a system in operational condition, and the physical state at deployment includes whatever inventory the system requires to function. Isotopic ratios in created rock are constitutive, carrying chronological weight only for post-fiat processes.

Distinguished from the older appearance-of-age proposal by two considerations: functional necessity of the radiogenic inventory for habitability, quantified by geoneutrino measurement, and the claim that the framing predicts cross-system concordance rather than merely surviving it.

## Status against the charter

This is simultaneously the programme's most developed position and its most exposed one.

The functional-necessity argument is in good shape and is quantitatively grounded. The concordance-prediction argument is not, and charter §8.3 now records the concession: integrated specification supports functional coherence among components, but deriving concordance of apparent ages across systems with different half-lives and geochemistry requires the demarcation criterion the programme does not have.

The line therefore rests on F-002 more heavily than any other, and the demarcation work area is the correct place for its next advance.

## Note on the declined auxiliary

Declining accelerated decay costs this line nothing, because functional maturity treats isotopic inventory as constitutive and incurs no obligation to compress elapsed decay at all. See [A-002](../registry/AUXILIARIES.md#a-002--accelerated-nuclear-decay) for the full grounds, including the quantitative statement of why the accelerated-decay thermal problem is a disposal constraint at the planetary radiative boundary rather than a transport problem.
