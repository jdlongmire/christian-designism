# I-0011 – Endogenous retroviruses as a specimen class for functional necessity

**Opened:** 2026-11-15
**Severity:** High. A candidate third specimen class for AD-001, with an unwelcome verdict, and a candidate rule for the criterion's open difficulty 3.
**Type:** Proposed application of the criterion, drafted by Claude for JD's decision. The demarcation page is unchanged.
**Related:** [F-002](../../03-registry/FALSIFIERS.md#f-002--continued-failure-to-supply-a-demarcation-criterion), [AD-001](../../03-registry/ADVANCEMENT.md#ad-001--demarcation-criterion-applied-in-advance-across-three-specimen-classes), [`02-programme/demarcation/`](../../02-programme/demarcation/), [I-0006](../open/I-0006-daughter-products-fail-functional-necessity.md), [I-0009](../open/I-0009-erv-model-copied-history-vs-hc8.md), [A-007](../../03-registry/AUXILIARIES.md#a-007--designed-template-copying-at-adams-formation)

## The application

Criterion (charter §7, v1.1): *a feature is constitutive if the system could not discharge its function at the moment of deployment without it.* Assignments stated for the class before asking what the framework would prefer:

| Feature | Verdict | Grounds |
|---|---|---|
| Target site duplication flanking a provirus | Elapsed history | No genomic function requires it; empty pre-integration sites carry one copy of the target sequence and occupied sites two (Barbulescu et al. 1999, 2001) |
| Disabled proviral ORFs; solo LTRs | Elapsed history | Damage and recombination products (Johnson & Coffin 1999; Hughes & Coffin 2004) |
| LTR divergence within a provirus | Elapsed history | Accumulated substitution; already assigned to elapsed history as the molecular-clock case |
| Functional ERV-derived sequence (syncytin env; MER41 enhancer) | Contested | The functional core may pass; its flanking duplication fails |

## Three consequences

**1. A structural parallel with I-0006.** A functional ERV splits as a radiogenic system does: the working part may pass while its attendant record fails. The two issues should be adjudicated on the same principle, and a ruling on one constrains the other.

**2. A candidate rule for open difficulty 3** (within-lineage accumulation against between-lineage difference). *Process signatures*, meaning features whose only known source is a process, mark elapsed history in whichever lineage they occur. Between-lineage differences without process signatures remain open to the initial-state reading. The rule is stateable, applicable, and it excludes things.

**3. An unwelcome verdict.** Shared human–ape ERVs carry process signatures. The criterion therefore assigns them to elapsed history shared across the human and ape genomes. That reproduces the I-0009 conflict with HC-8 from the programme's own criterion, independent of the ERV model's Layer 3.

## Effect on AD-001

If JD accepts the application and the process-signature rule, ERVs supply a third specimen class with at least one unwelcome verdict, applied before checking the framework's preference. AD-001's remaining conjunct, holding without reassignment, is then a matter of conduct over time. It should not be counted toward AD-001 until JD has decided this issue, since counting an undecided application is what the register exists to prevent.

## Status

**Resolved 2026-11-15.** JD accepted (a) the four assignments, (b) the process-signature rule as a candidate on the demarcation page, and (c) counting ERVs toward AD-001 as a specimen class with an unwelcome verdict. AD-001 remains `PARTIAL`: the conjunct "holds without reassignment" is a matter of conduct over time.
