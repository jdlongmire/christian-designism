# I-0007 – Three v1.0 additions not carried forward into v1.1

**Opened:** 2026-08-02
**Severity:** Medium for two of the three, low for the other. Author decision required.
**Type:** Editorial, not substantive disagreement.

## What happened

Charter v1.1 was prepared from the pre-repository draft rather than from the v1.0 deposit, so three substantive passages added at v1.0 are absent. They have not been reinstated, because silently re-inserting prose into a revision the author just produced would be an editorial override.

Each is listed with a recommendation. All three can be settled in one decision.

---

## 1. §6.1 relation between hydraulic collapse and catastrophic plate tectonics

**Recommend: restore.** This is the strongest of the three and the revision widens the gap it closed.

§6.1 presents catastrophic plate tectonics (Austin et al. 1994) as the geology line. But §7 registers hydration-texture predictions from Global Flood Hydrotectonics, §9 makes their absence a condition of abandonment, and v1.1 **adds** a third advancement condition resting on a hydrotectonic thermal budget. Three sections now depend on a model the survey section never introduces, and the two mechanisms are rivals: CPT drives motion by mantle thermal runaway, hydrotectonics by hydraulic seal failure with dissipation relocated into a water film. §7 also faults CPT's thermal budget while §6.1 presents it without qualification.

A reader who has only §6.1 cannot follow §7 or §9. This is an internal coherence defect rather than a matter of emphasis.

---

## 2. §7 quantitative statement of the radiative-boundary constraint

**Recommend: restore.** Cheap, and it converts an argument from well-reasoned to closed.

v1.1 §7 says compression "leaves the released energy unchanged and raises the power," which is correct and understated. The decisive point is that the constraint sits at the planetary radiative boundary rather than anywhere inside the Earth: shedding 10²⁹ to 10³⁰ J within a year requires the surface to radiate continuously at roughly 3,200 to 5,750 K against a silicate vaporization point near 3,000 K. That is why volumetric cooling had to be exotic, and stating it forecloses the standard reply that some better transport mechanism might yet be found.

v1.1's own §9 asserts "the heat problem defeated accelerated decay." The quantitative paragraph is what makes that assertion load-bearing rather than asserted.

The full grounds are retained in [A-002](../../registry/AUXILIARIES.md#a-002--accelerated-nuclear-decay) either way, so the argument is not lost to the programme if it stays out of the paper.

---

## 3. §8.3 concession on the concordance reply

**Recommend: restore, and strengthen.** v1.1 makes this more necessary, not less.

The v1.0 concession recorded that the functional-maturity account of isotopic concordance is a consistent interpretation rather than an earned prediction, pending the demarcation criterion. v1.1 restores the unqualified form: "concordance is entailed by the framework rather than absorbed by it."

Two developments in v1.1 cut against leaving it unqualified. The criterion now exists in candidate form, and applied to daughter products it appears to rule the other way ([I-0006](I-0006-daughter-products-fail-functional-necessity.md)) – so the reply at §8.3 is not merely ungrounded but may be contradicted by the programme's own new rule. And v1.1's new §8.5 concedes the symmetry principle that "the discriminating question in both directions is whether the adjustment predicted anything it was not built to explain," which applied to §8.3's first qualification gives the answer the concession stated.

Leaving §8.3 unqualified while §8.5 concedes the symmetry and §7 introduces a criterion that may contradict it is the one place a careful critic gets a clean shot at the paper's internal consistency.

## Note on workflow

The author revises from a local copy, so the repository will diverge on every revision unless one of two things happens: revisions are taken from the deposited version, or repository-side edits are confined to mechanical fixes and everything substantive is routed through issues like this one.

The second is the safer default and is what has been done here. Worth settling explicitly rather than rediscovering next revision.
