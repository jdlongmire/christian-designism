# I-0008 – Ordinal commitment, cardinal agnosticism: proposed hard-core amendment

**Opened:** 2026-08-02
**Severity:** High. Touches the hard core and reorganizes what several other entries are about.
**Type:** Proposed amendment, drafted for author decision. No charter text changed.
**Related:** HC-2, HC-5, HC-7, HC-9, [A-001](../../registry/AUXILIARIES.md#a-001--functional-maturity-of-origins), [A-005](../../registry/AUXILIARIES.md#a-005--anisotropic-synchrony-convention), [F-002](../../registry/FALSIFIERS.md#f-002--continued-failure-to-supply-a-demarcation-criterion), [I-0006](I-0006-daughter-products-fail-functional-necessity.md), charter §§1, 3, 6.2, 6.3, 6.5, 8.3

## Origin

Author clarification, 2026-08-02, arrived at across four successive sharpenings: from an agnosticism-about-age claim, to chronology rather than chronometry, to ordinality rather than cardinality, to the assertion that the ordinal/cardinal asymmetry is itself well evidenced.

The final form is the one worth amending on, because it is the only one that is both precise and checkable.

## The thesis

> The order type of a sequence of events is invariant. The cardinal measure on the intervals between them is not.

Formally: order type is preserved under any strictly monotonic reparametrization of the time coordinate; measure is not. Everything ordinal survives the map, everything cardinal is coordinate-dependent.

## Evidence, with scope

This section exists because the amendment must cite the physics at exactly the strength it supports and no further. Getting this wrong is how the passage would be lost.

**What is established, `HIGH` confidence, directly measured.**

Frame-dependence of interval magnitude is among the best-confirmed results in physics. Hafele and Keating flew caesium clocks around the world and recovered the predicted offsets at nanosecond scale (1972). Pound and Rebka measured gravitational redshift over a 22.5 m tower (1959). Chou and colleagues measured gravitational time dilation across a 33 cm height difference with optical clocks (2010). GPS requires a net correction of roughly 38 microseconds per day. Muon lifetimes dilate by exactly the Lorentz factor.

Order survives it: for timelike-separated events the sign of the interval is frame-invariant. This is a theorem, not an empirical accident. Observers disagree on duration and never on sequence.

**Two refinements the amendment must carry.**

The invariant structure is a **partial** order, not a total one. For spacelike-separated events the ordering genuinely does reverse between frames. The invariant object is the causal order, and Genesis presents a linear sequence, so the mapping needs a sentence.

The entropic arrow is a second and weaker line of support for ordinality being the more fundamental structure. Worth a clause, not worth leaning on.

**The scope limit, which is decisive.**

Relativistic variation is **lawful and computable, not indeterminate**. GPS works because the variation can be calculated to nanoseconds. Given a worldline and a metric, proper time along it is a definite number.

Consequently: **radiometric decay measures proper time along the same worldline as the rock.** Nuclide and host share a path through spacetime, and any co-moving clock agrees with any other by construction. Frame-dependence gives no leverage against the terrestrial radiometric case. Observers in different frames disagree about how long the Earth has existed, each can compute the other's answer, and proper time along Earth's own worldline is a single quantity they agree on. That quantity is what the geochronologist reports.

**Where it does reach.** Cosmological time. Comparing here-now with there-then across cosmic separations requires a simultaneity convention, and there the conventionality is structural rather than epistemic. Note that standard cosmology concedes rather than resists this: 13.8 Gyr is proper time along a comoving worldline in a specified foliation, which is explicitly foliation-relative. The honest claim is that the standard figure is well defined *given a stipulation*, which is weaker than arbitrary and stronger than settled.

**Therefore:** cite the physics at §6.5 where it applies. Do not cite it at §6.2. A charter that leans on relativistic time dilation to support the geochronological position loses the passage the moment a critic holds up GPS.

## Counter-evidence to hold rather than meet later

Independent cardinal methods cross-calibrate. Dendrochronology, varve counts, ice-core layers and radiocarbon agree across roughly the last fifty thousand years, from physically unrelated processes. That is direct evidence for cardinal stability over that span, produced by exactly the convergence the programme's own consilience criterion demands.

It does not extend to billions of years, and pressing that limitation is legitimate. Over its range it is real, and it is the first thing a competent critic reaches for.

## What the framing does not dissolve

**HC-9 is a pure ordinal claim in conflict with the standard ordering.** Death enters the human line after Adam; the standard sequence places death ordinally prior to humans by a large margin. No monotonic reparametrization touches this, because reparametrization preserves order and the conflict is about order. The same holds for humans-early against humans-late.

So the framing **localizes** the dispute rather than removing it, and localizes it well. The age question becomes the negotiable part. The irreducible part is an ordering conflict, which is smaller and more tractable than arguing about billions of years.

**This settles the §6.3 question.** The programme cannot concede the column's ordering and dispute only its metrication, because HC-9 forbids it. §6.3 must dispute the ordering, and now there is a principled reason rather than a convenient one: flood sorting is the claim that the column's apparent ordinal structure is spatial and hydrodynamic rather than temporal. That is the right shape of claim for an ordinally-committed programme, and it is where the programme's weight should go.

## The three-way dilemma

State the relation between sequence and measure carelessly and it collapses one of two ways.

| Option | Status |
|---|---|
| **Purely conventional** reparametrization, a coordinate relabeling | Preserves every observation and therefore has no physical content. Delivers a young *description* of the same Earth. The dispute becomes verbal. |
| **Physical** reparametrization, rates actually differed | Accelerated decay under another name. The radiative-boundary problem of [A-002](../../registry/AUXILIARIES.md#a-002--accelerated-nuclear-decay) returns intact. |
| **Non-integrable fiat boundary** | Neither convention nor rate change. The claim that the measure does not integrate across a deployment boundary, so summing intervals across one is not a defined operation. |

The third is the only option that is both non-empty and heat-free, and it is what functional maturity already asserts.

**It must be labelled as revelation-sourced, not as continuous with the relativistic result.** Relativity supplies smooth lawful reparametrization along worldlines and never breaks the integral; proper time remains ∫dτ throughout. Nothing in the evidence above supports a boundary across which the measure fails to integrate.

This is a strength rather than a concession. HC-5 already licenses exactly this class of boundary condition and §3 already explains why such conditions cannot be read off the data. Dressing it in relativity would import a defeater the bare claim does not carry.

**And it puts the whole weight back on F-002.** The ordinal framing does not relieve the demarcation problem. It makes it the entire game, and explains why: if the position is that the measure does not integrate across fiat boundaries, then everything depends on knowing where the boundaries are and how a specimen betrays one.

## Effect on I-0006

The severity drops. If daughter products read as elapsed history, an ordinally-committed programme can absorb it: the ratio measures a post-deployment interval whose metrication across the fiat boundary is precisely the disputed operation. This is relief from severity, not from the question, and the question migrates from geochronology into F-002 where it was always going to be decided.

# Drafted amendment

Text below is ready to lift. Nothing has been applied.

## 1. Hard core

**Amend HC-2** from categorical to graded:

> **HC-2. Framework dependence of historical reconstruction, graded by auxiliary load.** Reconstruction of unrepeated past events requires auxiliary assumptions concerning initial conditions, system closure, rate constancy across the reconstructed interval, and the absence of singular intervening events. These are supplied by the governing framework and are not read off the data. Warrant varies inversely with the number and strength of the assumptions required to bridge trace to event, and with the opportunity for unmodeled singular intervention across the interval. Temporal distance from the event correlates with both and constitutes neither.

**Add HC-12:**

> **HC-12. Ordinal invariance, cardinal variability.** The order type of a historical sequence is the epistemically robust structure; the cardinal measure on its intervals is not. Ordinal warrant is high but not auxiliary-free, since correlation of sequences across separated systems carries theoretical commitments of its own. Cardinal assignment additionally requires a metric, and across a fiat boundary the framework holds that the measure does not integrate.

**Add to the amendment-history rule's disclosure**, since `hard-core.md` requires any addition to name the pressure that prompted it:

> HC-12 was added following author clarification rather than under anomaly pressure, and it **narrows** the programme's commitments rather than protecting a threatened one. Additions that reduce what is claimed are not the manoeuvre the Lakatosian criterion targets. Recorded here so that the distinction is on the record rather than asserted later.

## 2. Charter §3, new subsection after the rate-constancy paragraph

> **Ordinality and cardinality.** The distinction between an ordered sequence and a measure on its intervals is the sharpest available form of the point just made, and it is independently evidenced. Interval magnitude is frame-dependent, which is among the best-confirmed results in physics: Hafele and Keating (1972) recovered the predicted offsets from circumnavigating caesium clocks at nanosecond scale, Pound and Rebka (1959) measured gravitational redshift over a 22.5 metre tower, and Chou et al. (2010) measured gravitational time dilation across a 33 centimetre height difference. Satellite navigation requires the corresponding correction or fails within hours. Temporal order between timelike-separated events, by contrast, is frame-invariant as a matter of theorem: observers disagree about duration and never about sequence. The invariant object is a partial order rather than a total one, since ordering between spacelike-separated events is genuinely frame-dependent.
>
> Two limits on what this establishes should be stated plainly, because the claim is easily overdrawn. The variation is lawful and computable rather than indeterminate; given a worldline and a metric, proper time along it is a definite quantity. And a decay clock shares the worldline of its host mineral, so co-moving clocks agree by construction and frame-dependence supplies no leverage against terrestrial radiometric measurement. The result bears on the comparison of separated events, which is a cosmological matter treated in Section 6.5, and not on the duration of a single worldline.
>
> What the distinction does establish is where auxiliary load concentrates. Ordering is recoverable from nested structure, superposition and causal dependency at comparatively low assumption cost. Metrication requires rate constancy across the unobserved interval, which is the third of the three claims separated above and the one that is assumed rather than measured. The programme's commitments are accordingly ordinal, and its position on cardinal assignment is developed in Sections 6.2 and 6.5.

## 3. Charter §6.2, replacing the concordance argument

Replaces "The second is that this framing predicts cross-system concordance..." and supersedes the §8.3 first qualification.

> The second consideration concerns cross-system concordance, and the account offered here differs from the appearance-of-age tradition and from the earlier statement of this paper. Concordance among independent isotopic systems is a cardinal fact: magnitudes agreeing. On an ordinal commitment it requires no separate explanation from design coherence, which never derived it. It follows instead from there being a single relation between the sequence and its measure. If one map relates order to metric, every clock reparametrizes together and concordance is preserved trivially. Discordance would be the surprising result, since it would require the systems to be related to the underlying order by different maps.
>
> This is a genuine derivation where the earlier appeal to integrated specification was not, and it comes at a price worth stating. Concordance so explained is evidence for the *unity* of the relation between sequence and measure. It is not evidence for any particular cardinal value, in either direction.

## 4. Charter §6.5, added framing

> The conventionality at issue here is not local to this problem. It is the general thesis of Section 3 applied to the one domain in which physics already concedes it: causal order is invariant, simultaneity and duration across spacelike separation are convention-relative. The standard cosmological figure is proper time along a comoving worldline in a specified foliation, and is therefore well defined given a stipulation rather than independent of one. An anisotropic synchrony convention is a different stipulation, not a departure from the practice of stipulating.

## 5. Charter §1, scope paragraph

The current paragraph commits to "six ordinary days" and "thousands of years," which are cardinal claims. **This is the decision the author has to make and the amendment cannot make for him.**

Two readings are available:

**(a) Strict ordinal.** The biblical durations are the text's own reckoning, functioning as ordering data with internal interval relations, and the framework takes no position on their mapping to external physical metric. Fully consistent, and it costs §1's exclusion claim: the framework would no longer exclude a multi-billion-year physical-metric Earth, only deny that the metric integrates across the boundaries the chronology names.

**(b) Reckoning-local cardinal.** The durations are cardinal claims *at the deployment locus*, in the text's own reckoning, combined with the denial that external metric integrates across fiat boundaries. Preserves "six ordinary days" as a duration claim about the interval it names while leaving cross-boundary summation undefined.

Reading (b) appears to be what the framework holds and is the more natural fit with HC-7. It should be stated explicitly, because the difference is exactly what a critic will probe.

## What adopting this costs and retires

**Retires.** The weakest argument in the charter. The "integrated specification produces coherent components" account of concordance has been flagged three times and never derived what it claimed; §6.2 above replaces it with a derivation. It also unifies §6.5, which currently reads as special pleading and would read as the clearest instance of a general principle.

**Costs.** §1 must choose between readings (a) and (b) and say so. The programme becomes explicitly committed to an ordinal conflict at HC-9 that it cannot reparametrize away, which is more exposed and more honest. And §6.3 acquires a defined job: to argue that the column's apparent ordering is spatial rather than temporal. That is a harder brief than the one it currently carries, though it is now a principled one.

**Does not change.** F-002 remains `UNMET` and becomes more load-bearing rather than less. I-0006 drops in severity without being answered.
