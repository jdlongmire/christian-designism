# I-0009 – ERV model: copied history across the human–ape boundary conflicts with HC-8

**Opened:** 2026-11-15
**Severity:** High. Self-generated, and it bears on a hard-core commitment.
**Related:** [HC-8](../../programme/hard-core.md), [P-006](../../registry/PREDICTIONS.md#p-006--h2b-exogenization-test-e5-foamy-virus-lineage), [`lines/genetics.md`](../../lines/genetics.md)
**Blocks:** Extension of the ERV model to human lineages. Does **not** block P-006.

## Problem

The ERV explanatory model under methodological designism (working outline v0.4, not yet in a repository of record) has three relevant features:

1. **Layer 3** accepts copied genomic history wherever shared, tree-congruent markers run.
2. Humans and chimpanzees share ERVs at orthologous loci with shared target site duplications. For example, ERV-W orthologs are present in chimpanzees at 205 of 211 human loci (Grandi et al., as cited in Jorritsma 2022), and HERV-K-GC1 shows the shared-insertion signature among the African apes (Barbulescu et al. 2001).
3. The model has retired every account of shared ERVs that does not involve copying: independent targeting of integration hotspots, creation in place, and originally site-specific integration (outline §§4, 7).

Taken together, these imply that the human genome shares copied history with the chimpanzee lineage. If that copying was by reproduction, it contradicts HC-8 on the reading the programme holds: Adam formed directly (Genesis 2:7), and not descended from a prior lineage. If the programme's reading of HC-8 is narrower than this, that should be stated in the hard core, since this issue depends on it.

This is **not** an instance of [F-001](../../registry/FALSIFIERS.md#f-001--formal-contradiction-between-load-bearing-commitments). F-001 concerns contradiction between hard-core commitments, and the ERV model is a candidate auxiliary. Under the negative heuristic the auxiliary yields, so the model cannot be adopted as stated.

## Why this is load-bearing

The genetics line has no model of its own beyond mutational load (P-004). The ERV model is the first candidate genetics auxiliary that generates registrable content (P-006). If it can be adopted only by contradicting HC-8, the programme's only registered prediction would come from a model it cannot hold. A critic would be right to say so, and the programme should say it first.

## Resolution paths

**R1. Designed template copying at Adam's formation.** The copying across the human–ape boundary was performed by the Designer, reusing a template genome, and was not reproduction. This stays inside HC-8 and matches the outline's own premise that designed copying and reproductive copying leave the same static pattern. Costs, all to be stated in advance:
- The template's insertion history (shared target site duplications, shared post-insertion mutations, LTR divergence, the GC1 sorting pattern) must have accumulated in the template lineage before Adam's formation. Under HC-6 and a young timeframe that compresses the history into a short pre-Adamic interval. HC-9 places the Fall's introduction of death in the *human* line, so corruption in a non-human template lineage before the Fall needs its own exegetical account.
- It reopens the appearance-of-history question for the human case. Adam's genome would carry the record of insertion events that happened in another lineage. That history is real in the template but was never lived by Adam, which is the Omphalos objection the outline elsewhere avoids.

**R2. A human-specific non-copying account.** Find an account of human–ape shared ERVs that survives the evidence that retired the alternatives (empty pre-integration sites, nested shared post-insertion changes, present-day integration behaviour). No candidate is currently known.

**R3. Restrict the model's scope.** Adopt the ERV model only for non-human lineages, and record human–ape shared ERVs as an open anomaly for the programme. Honest, but it leaves the strongest mainstream ERV datum unaddressed and counts against coverage in the outline's parity scorecard.

**R5. Common deployment (added 2026-11-15).** Shared human–ape loci were deployed by design at instantiation into kinds sharing architecture; lineage-specific loci are post-Fall. No template lineage and no pre-Adamic history are needed, so R1's two costs fall away. Its own costs are listed at [A-010](../../registry/AUXILIARIES.md#a-010--common-deployment-two-population-erv-model), chiefly the reassignment of a specimen class (AD-001), shared lesions, the LTR gradient and the unidentified deployment variable.

**R4. Decline the model.** Record it in [`registry/AUXILIARIES.md`](../../registry/AUXILIARIES.md) as declined, with grounds, as A-002 was.

## What resolution requires

1. An author decision among R1–R4.
2. If R1: an exegetical note on template reuse at Adam's formation, and a written treatment of the appearance-of-history cost, entered here before the model is adopted.
3. In every case: the model deposited in a repository of record, and [`lines/genetics.md`](../../lines/genetics.md) updated.

## Positions

**Claude (raised the issue, 2026-11-15): R3 now; R1 only if it passes a novelty test.**
- R1 costs more than it first appears. HERV-K-GC1 is present at the identical base pair in gorilla, chimpanzee and bonobo and absent in humans (Barbulescu et al. 2001). That sorting pattern requires a template *population* with its own history, which gave rise to gorillas and chimpanzees before Adam's formation. In the charter's young timeframe this is a pre-Adamic primate population history compressed into the sixth day, and it pushes kind width toward a broad ape kind.
- R1 currently predicts nothing the mainstream programme does not. Adopted now, under pressure and without excess content, it would be ad hoc₁, the signature the charter uses to decline A-002. The programme cannot decline RATE on that ground and then adopt R1 on the same footing.
- R3 is honest about the cost: shared human–ape ERVs stay an open anomaly, which lowers coverage on the parity scorecard.
